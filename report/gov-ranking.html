What this means, in one paragraph. If a domain publishes no DMARC record, receiving mail servers get no instruction at all about mail claiming to come from it — so it is delivered. If DMARC is set to p=none, spoofed mail is detected but still delivered. Only p=reject makes receiving servers refuse it. A domain with SPF ending in ~all labels spoofed mail instead of refusing it. Each of these is one TXT record, and the safe order is the same everywhere: quarantine first, read the reports, then reject.

How the rollout works

; step 1 - watch, do not block
_dmarc.example.gov.  IN TXT "v=DMARC1; p=quarantine; rua=mailto:dmarc@example.gov"

; step 2 - after 2-4 weeks, when reports only list senders you recognise
_dmarc.example.gov.  IN TXT "v=DMARC1; p=reject; rua=mailto:dmarc@example.gov"

; step 3 - only after every legitimate sender is listed in SPF
example.gov.         IN TXT "v=spf1 include:_spf.google.com -all"

Doing step 3 before step 1 is the single most common way a DMARC rollout gets rolled back: one forgotten sender stops sending and mail disappears silently.
